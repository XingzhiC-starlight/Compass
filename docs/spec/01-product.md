## §1 Product

Code: `src/`

**One-liner.** A focus and pacing tool that makes a person's progress, time and motivation
visible to themselves, so they can keep building something hard at a pace that is both kind
and honest.

**The problem.** People building long, self-directed projects with no deadline and no boss lose
momentum not because they are disorganized, but because they cannot see where they are.
Progress feels invisible, effort feels infinite, and uncertainty turns every unfamiliar task
into a fresh reason not to start.

**Value proposition.** Compass removes the fog. It is an orientation instrument, not a task
manager: it tells the user "you are here", gives them one honest starting point each day,
holds a space for thinking on the page when they get stuck, and accumulates a track record
that proves progress is real. The building work itself happens outside the tool.

**Who it is for.** A non-technical founder or maker on a tiny self-funded team, building an
app or technical product out of self-interest with no external deadline, and learning to code
while building. They visit the tool to spin up a work session, to regroup when stuck, and to
log what they did — they do not live in it. The tool is for the individual, not the team.

**Core thesis and mental model.** The product manages the user's self-perception, not their
tasks. Its mechanics are the evidence-based ones — behavioral activation, cognitive reframing,
planning-fallacy correction, and implementation intentions (which is what the interview is) —
rather than generic productivity features. It measures two quantities — *time* (how much the user has, spends and needs) and
*progress through uncertainty* (where they stand on a foggy, learn-as-you-go path) — and
supports one process: *start → sustain → accumulate*.

**Shape of the product.**

- One project, one dashboard. Every live project sits in the sidebar as a pill, so the whole
  portfolio is visible and switching pills is the multi-project view (§6).
- A one-time interview sets a project up; answering it is itself the cognitive warm-up (§13).
- The dashboard has a fixed, ritual layout: three orientation bubbles above — time
  commitment, projected timeline, learning — sharing one summary ⇄ depth switch, and the
  always-live action panel below — daily tasks, with the thought log behind a toggle (§7–§12).
- Signed-out visitors see a public landing page (§15); accounts use Google or email (§5).

**Design principles** — the guardrails for every UI decision:

1. **Orientation, not management.** Closer to a compass or map than a to-do list.
2. **Calm surface, depth on demand** — for the three upper bubbles. The Daily Task panel is
   the deliberate exception: full detail, in-place editing, alive by design.
3. **Fixed and ritual.** Same layout every visit. Consistency over customization.
4. **Honest and kind at once.** The screen must hold both "be realistic" and "be gentle with
   yourself" without them fighting.
5. **Never punish a hard, honest day.** Learning days, re-plans, short-hour days and downward
   percentage changes must feel like the system working, not like failure.
6. **Percentages in the learning panel are self-rated vibes, not measurements.** They are
   allowed to go down. Without this, the tool quietly punishes honesty.
7. **Scaffold, then fade.** The tool supports heavily early and should reduce its own
   scaffolding as the user builds their own engine.
8. **Visit, not live-in.** Success is the focused work that happens after the user leaves.

**What success looks like.** A user independently sustains work on a hard project over
months; a visit reliably converts into a focused work session; the user develops a realistic
self-perception of time and progress. Success is not time in the app.
